AdministrateMe V3

Connect everything. Write policy as you go.

The queue isn't too full because too much arrives. It's too full because no policy exists yet. Two moments write that policy — connecting a source, and zeroing the queue — and they are the same act performed at different times.

1. The root problem

Not "we forget things." That's the symptom, and building for the symptom is how you get 462 undecided items.

Things that need doing arrive scattered across many places. The cost of moving one into where you'll actually look is higher than the thing feels worth in the moment. So it doesn't get moved. So it's forgotten. And the pile of unmoved things becomes its own dread, which makes you avoid it, which makes it grow.

The bottleneck, named precisely, is the human decision, per item. Which gives the only test that matters:

Does the number of decisions you make go down as a fraction of what arrives? If not, the system fails — however good its capture, extraction or instrumentation.

V2's capture is excellent — source accounting across 64,000 records, per-source freshness, "why is this here" on every row, observation mode. And it automated arrival without touching the cost of departure. Decisions-per-item is still 1.0.

2. The wrong fix, and why

I proposed restricting the input — a shared inbox you forward things to. It's wrong, and the reason is worth stating because it rules out a whole family of "just connect less" answers.

Forwarding fails on exactly the failure mode the system exists to fix. The thing you forget to forward is the thing you were going to forget anyway. A capture mechanism that depends on remembering to capture is not a fix for forgetting — it's the same bet, made twice.

Every variant has the same flaw. Connect one label, one folder, one VIP list: each one is a rule you have to author in advance, about senders you haven't met yet, at the moment when you know the least.

3. The real fix: policy density

462 isn't an input problem. It's a policy problem. V2 has zero rules, so every item is a first encounter, forever. The same twelve senders get re-decided every week.

The lever is not how much you let in. It is what fraction of what arrives is already covered by something you decided earlier.

Why this works arithmetically. Mail volume is dominated by repeat senders — vendors, notifications, newsletters, the same dozen people. A decision about a sender is worth hundreds of decisions about items. Policy density climbs fast at first because you're covering the fat head of the distribution, then slowly, because what's left is the genuinely novel tail. That tail is what triage is for, and it is small.

4. One mechanism, two moments

This is the whole design, and it's why the system is simpler than it looks.

One component, two entry points. If you find yourself building two grouping engines, stop — you've split something that should be shared.

5. The New Connection Wizard

The piece I missed entirely, and the one that makes connecting everything safe. It runs once per connection and its job is to end with policy already written.

Step 3, wireframed — the screen that does the work

Four buttons per sender — the same four as triage. Six decisions here removed roughly 900 future ones.

The rule that keeps the wizard honest. It uses behaviour, not content, to rank and describe — how many, whether you ever replied, whether you ever opened it. Never "this looks unimportant." Behavioural evidence is checkable by you in one glance; a model's opinion about importance is not, and you'd have no way to catch it being wrong.

One rules table, two origins. If the wizard wrote to a different table than zeroing, you'd have two policy systems that disagree — and you'd find out months later when one of them stopped applying.

The extraction contract — the entire AI surface

{
  "kind":  "calendar" | "reminder" | "file" | "drop",
  "title": "Flight DL2411 to Vancouver",
  "when":  "2026-08-28T06:15:00-04:00",   // null when there is no time
  "list":  "Personal",
  "why":   "Delta confirmation with a departure time",
  "sure":  true                            // false = ask, don't pre-fill
}
Never invent a date. No time in the text means when is null and it becomes a reminder, not a calendar entry. A plausible guessed date is worse than no date, because you'll act on it.

10. How to build it — six weeks

Order matters more than speed. The constraint that sets it: you cannot safely connect a real inbox until the wizard exists. So weeks 1–3 build the machine against a deliberately tiny scope, and week 4 is what unlocks everything else.

WeekBuildEnd state
1members · connections · items · decisions · pushes. One connection, scoped to the last 7 days of one label. Triage screen, four buttons. Push to Google Calendar and Reminders. No AI.The machine works end to end against a handful of items. Nothing can drown you.
2The single model call. The card pre-fills.Two seconds an item instead of thirty.
3rules · the always-checkbox · rules applied at Gate 2 · grouping · retroactive sweep · Rules screen.Policy exists and works. This is the product.
4The New Connection Wizard, reusing week 3's grouping component pointed at history.You can now connect a real inbox without drowning.
5Connect for real, one at a time, wizard each: Gmail → Outlook → iMessage → WhatsApp. Laura's connections after yours.Everything is in. Policy density climbs with each one.
6Plaid patterns · Notes · Today screen · duplicate detection across connections.Obligations you never wrote down, and todos buried in your own prose.
Why no AI in week 1. If the model never works you still have a working system. Every dependency deferred past the first useful version is one that can't sink it.
The sequencing rule that protects you. One new connection at a time, and don't start the next until the last one's queue has been zeroed once. Connect four at once and you have no way to tell which one is generating the noise — and iMessage and WhatsApp go last, after the person-to-person bar has been proven against real transactional volume.

11. What we are not building

Not buildingBecause
A forwarding inboxDepends on remembering to capture, which is the thing that's broken.
A knowledge graph engineRelations that matter are links between items. Store a link; don't build a graph.
Confidence classes, readiness states, source accountingExcellent for many consumers of one truth. You have one consumer — you.
A People directoryPeople emerge from senders, and the wizard already ranks them by how you behave toward them.
Comms and Scoreboard surfacesYour mail clients are the comms surface. Progress is the bar at the top of triage.
Cross-member browsingMembers share destinations, never inboxes. That's what makes connecting everything acceptable.

12. The test to hold it to

Two numbers, weekly.

Policy coverage — what share of arrivals were handled without you. Should climb steeply, then flatten high.
Decisions per week — should fall while volume stays flat or rises.

If coverage is flat and decisions aren't falling, the learning loop is broken, and nothing else matters until it isn't.

Every part of this exists to move those two numbers. The wizard front-loads coverage; zeroing grows it; Gate 2 spends it. Anything proposed later that doesn't move them — however clever — is elaboration, and elaboration is what produced 462.

Connect everything. Decide about senders, not messages. Let the wizard and the zeroing write the same rules into the same table. That's the system.
